JIAN GAO

gaojian21@mails.tsinghua.edu.cn

Beijing, China

I am a Ph.D. student at the College of AI, Tsinghua University, advised by Prof. Ji Wu and Prof. Miao Li. I received my bachelor’s degrees in Mathematics and Physics and in Energy and Power Engineering from Weiyang College, Tsinghua University.

My research asks what large language and multimodal models actually learn during training, and how to steer it.

  • Post-training and trust in external evidence. Retrieved passages, tool outputs and upstream models are usually right but sometimes wrong. I study how supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning (GRPO) change whether a multimodal model follows such external evidence or its own perception when the two conflict, and which reinforced samples drive the behavior.
  • Knowledge learning. Large and small models attend to different parts of the same text. The difference between their attention reveals elusive but important clues, and amplifying these clues through data augmentation improves knowledge learning for both large and small models (AAAI 2025, Oral).
  • Priors as training signals. When scarce data fit both the intended rule and a misleading one, task-level natural-language priors can be turned into contrastive training objectives rather than prompts (Prior-Guided Tuning).
  • Clinical AI. With orthopedic surgeons, I work on retrieval-augmented question answering and on multimodal decision support for spine surgery, where much of the work lies in auditing labels and turning clinical questions into testable ones.
